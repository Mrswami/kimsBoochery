# Kim's Boochery — Enterprise Scale-Up & Beverage Production Roadmap

This roadmap outlines the operational, technological, and legal stages required to transition Kim's Boochery from a farmers market kiosk into a high-volume, commercially distributed beverage brand.

---

## Phase 1: Recipe Standardization & Stabilizing (The Science)
To scale kombucha or craft soda to wholesale distribution (HEB, local grocers, convenience stores), you must guarantee shelf-stability and consistent taste profiles.

1. **ABV Control & Stabilizing:**
   * Commercial kombucha must stay strictly under **0.5% ABV** to avoid TABC / TTB regulation.
   * **Solution:** Transition from traditional long-term open fermentation to controlled temperature jacketed tanks. Implement pasteurization (sterile filtration) or centrifugal yeast separation to halt secondary fermentation in the bottle.
2. **Forced Carbonation:**
   * Rather than bottle-conditioning (which produces sediment and rises ABV), brew flat kombucha, chill it to 34°F, and carbonate it under pressure using a CO2 stone in a brite tank. This produces cleaner, more consistent soda-like carbonation.
3. **Lab Analysis & Nutritional Labels:**
   * Send samples to an ISO-certified food testing lab to generate official **Nutrition Facts panels** (required for retail) and verify shelf life.

---

## Phase 2: High-Volume Production Systems (The Hardware)
Moving from 5-gallon carboys to industrial batches requires specialized machinery:

| Equipment Category | Small Scale (Kiosk) | Mid Scale (Micro-Brewery) | Industrial Scale (Soda Plant) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Fermentation** | Glass carboys & plastic bins | 10–30 Barrel Stainless Jacketed Conical Tanks | 100+ Barrel Jacketed Fermenters |
| **Carbonation** | Natural bottle carbonation | Brite Tank with CO2 Diffusion Stones | Automated Inline Carbonation System |
| **Packaging** | Manual handheld bottle filler | Semi-automatic 4-head counter-pressure filler | Automated monobloc canning/bottling rotary line |
| **Lab Testing** | Handheld refractometer / pH pen | Anton Paar alcohol analyzer | Full in-house lab (GC-MS, PCR testing) |

---

## Phase 3: Commercial Manufacturing Compliance
To sell outside of farmers markets, you must transition from Cottage Food limits to commercial food facilities.

1. **Leasing a Commercial Kitchen or Incubator Space:**
   * Start by renting shared time in a certified incubator kitchen (e.g., commercial prep spaces in Austin).
   * As volume grows, lease a dedicated industrial warehouse space zoned for light manufacturing.
2. **HACCP (Hazard Analysis Critical Control Point) Plan:**
   * Required for juice and fermented beverage production. You must document sanitation protocols, pH levels, pasteurization times, and cold-chain compliance.
3. **FDA & DSHS Registration:**
   * Register the production facility with the FDA (for interstate commerce) and obtain a **Food Manufacturer License** from the Texas Department of State Health Services (DSHS).

---

## Phase 4: The Co-Packing Shortcut (Asset-Light Scaling)
Building a bottling plant is highly capital-intensive ($500k–$2M+). The fastest way to scale to grocery store volume is using a **Co-Packer (Contract Manufacturer)**:

* **How it works:** You supply the recipe, brand assets, and packaging specifications. The co-packer mixes, ferments, pasteurizes, cans, and cartons the beverage in their facility.
* **Benefits:** Immediately gain access to high-speed canning lines, pasteurizers, and QA labs without upfront capital expenditures.
* **Process:** Identify a co-packer in Texas that handles carbonated beverages/acidified foods and run a test batch (usually 1,000–5,000 cases).

---

## Phase 5: Distribution & Sales Channels
1. **Direct Store Delivery (DSD):**
   * Partner with local beverage distributors (specializing in craft sodas/beverages) to stock regional grocery stores and cafes.
2. **ERP Inventory Management:**
   * Integrate an ERP (like Katana or Odoo) to track batch raw ingredients, lot numbers (essential for recalls), and packaging materials.
